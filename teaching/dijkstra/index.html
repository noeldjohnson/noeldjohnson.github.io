Dijkstra's Algorithm Step by Step

Noel D. Johnson · Spatial Techniques for Empirical Economics · George Mason University

1A six-node network

Each line is a road with a cost of travel. Dijkstra's algorithm finds the cheapest cost from one starting node to every other node. It keeps a tentative cost for every node and a set of settled nodes. At each step it settles the unsettled node with the lowest tentative cost and then relaxes that node's roads. Relaxing a road means lowering a neighbor's tentative cost when the route through the settled node is cheaper.

A settled node's cost never changes again. When it is settled every other unsettled node already costs at least as much, and with costs that are never negative no later route can come back cheaper. That is why the algorithm needs nonnegative costs.

2A raster is a graph

Every cell of a raster is a node and every cell touches its eight neighbors. The cost of a step between two cells is the mean of their costs times the distance between their centers, so a diagonal step costs 1.41 times a straight one. This is what gdistance builds with transition and geoCorrection, and the algorithm is the same as above. Paint cells with a mode of transport, then find the cheapest route.
